## Player-facing contract

In gameplay, pressing the configured Attack button still starts Attack immediately. Without lifting, sliding that thumb turns the camera exactly like swiping the ordinary look area, including after sliding outside the button. Stopping the drag leaves Attack held; lifting releases it once. A stationary press or tap must not move the camera. This follows the Attack action even when the player moves or resizes its button.

A dedicated free-look finger takes precedence while present, whether it landed before or after Attack. Attack stays held. Only one finger contributes camera movement at a time. When the look finger lifts, the Attack thumb can aim again on fresh motion, with no jump or replay of suppressed travel. The movement thumb stays independent. Cancel, mode change, interruption and input-device takeover must clear affected ownership without a stuck action or late delta.

## Separate observation: possible input lag

The user identifies **swiping empty space to turn the camera** on the shipped WO40 build as the clearest example. No duration or cause is measured. The adapter queues ordinary look on the move callback, but rounds each scaled delta and emits nothing when both axes round to zero. This can discard tiny motions; it does not prove a time delay. If the user compares ordinary look and Attack-drag in the same scene, record the observation separately. Do not change ordinary look, add latency instrumentation, launch a benchmark, tune display/VSync or spend another build on this report. It is not a prerequisite for delivering the Attack fix.
